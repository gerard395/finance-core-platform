# W1-003E – Relation Classification Read Persistence

## Status and resolved blocker

**Implemented.** W1-003E0 resolved the former ambiguity by adding required immutable `OpenItemType::Receivable` and `OpenItemType::Payable` as Accounting source truth. A Relation may still independently be Customer, Supplier, both or neither. Customer/Supplier presence never determines an OpenItem's financial side.

## Domain inventory and hydration

`Relation` is the aggregate root for shared counterparty data. Its factual persisted basis is immutable `RelationId` and normalized immutable `RelationCode`, mutable `DisplayName`, and active state changed through explicit Domain methods. It has no `AdministrationId`. Contacts, Addresses and BankAccounts are owned child collections but are outside this persistence foundation; the Relation adapter refuses saving a non-empty child collection rather than silently losing it.

`Customer` and `Supplier` are independent classification entities. Customer has immutable `CustomerId`, `RelationId` and normalized `CustomerNumber`; Supplier analogously has `SupplierId`, `RelationId` and `SupplierNumber`. Both have explicitly mutable active state. Their existing constructors accept complete factual state, as does Relation, so Eloquent hydration uses only typed value objects and those constructors—no reflection, persistence setters, lifecycle replay or Domain framework dependency.

## Tenant ownership and schema

Relation remains unchanged. Following the established LedgerAccount pattern, persistence assigns every globally unique Relation identity to exactly one Administration and every read requires `AdministrationId`.

Three ordered reversible migrations add:

- `relations`: UUID primary identity, required Administration owner, code, display name, active state and timestamps; tenant/code uniqueness, tenant/identity candidate key and tenant/name read index;
- `customers`: UUID primary identity, Administration, Relation identity, customer number, active state and timestamps; one Customer classification per tenant/Relation and tenant/number uniqueness;
- `suppliers`: the analogous Supplier facts and uniqueness constraints.

Customer and Supplier use composite `(administration_id, relation_id)` foreign keys to Relations, so cross-tenant classification is rejected even outside adapters. There is deliberately no Customer/Supplier exclusivity constraint. All references restrict deletion. The Relations migration also replaces the formerly deferred OpenItem Relation reference with a restrictive same-tenant composite foreign key; financial history cannot be cascade-deleted. The required-constraint precondition found zero development `open_items` records.

## Application contracts and adapters

The narrow tenant-scoped contracts are:

- `RelationReadRepository::findByIdForAdministration()` and `findForAdministration()`;
- `RelationStore::save()` for mutable name/active state with immutable identity/code and ownership protection;
- Customer/Supplier read repositories with `findForAdministration()` and `existsForRelation()`;
- Customer/Supplier stores with `save()` for active state while preserving immutable identity, Relation and number;
- `RelationClassificationReader::classify()` returning typed `RelationClassification` with `isCustomer()` and `isSupplier()`.

`EloquentRelationRepository`, `EloquentCustomerRepository`, `EloquentSupplierRepository` and `EloquentRelationClassificationReader` implement and are bound to these ports. There is no generic base repository, global `findAll()`, cross-tenant lookup or delete contract. Reads are deterministically ordered and always scoped in SQL. Classification means persisted classification presence; active state remains separately available on the hydrated entity.

## Overlap and financial independence

The same tenant/Relation can have one Customer row and one Supplier row simultaneously. Either row can also exist alone, or neither can exist. The classification reader reports those four factual states and cannot see another tenant's records.

This capability is for relation management and future party display/filtering only. Financial classification follows exclusively:

`OpenItemReadRepository → OpenItem::type()`

- Receivable contributes to `outstandingReceivables`;
- Payable contributes to `outstandingPayables`.

Relation persistence supplies counterparty identity and future name/detail data. W1-004 needs no Customer/Supplier lookup, amount sign, ledger account, JournalEntry type or Presentation document heuristic. A dual-classified Relation can own both a Receivable and a Payable OpenItem without ambiguity.

## Verification coverage

Infrastructure integration tests cover Relation roundtrip and mutable state, immutable identity/code and ownership, Customer/Supplier roundtrip and active state, all four classification combinations, overlap, A/B isolation, cross-tenant database rejection, duplicate classification rejection, restrictive Relation deletion, service-container bindings and explicit OpenItemType independence.
